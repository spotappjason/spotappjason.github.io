Article 1 · What this covers

This policy covers the Spot application for macOS and the website at spotappjason.github.io, including its download, pricing, changelog, privacy and terms pages. It does not cover Google, Supabase, GitHub, Cloudflare, Voyage AI, Anthropic, OpenAI or any other service you reach through Spot; their own policies apply to them, and they are named below.

Article 2 · What stays on your Mac

  • Your references. The pictures, links and the sentences you write about them are files in a folder on your computer that you choose. Spot reads and organises that folder. Capture and import can copy or download pictures, and filing can move them. There is no cloud vault mirror in the current app. AI Describe sends pictures to a provider; text meaning features send reference text and queries as described in Articles 4 and 5.
  • Your AI keys. If you connect Claude (Anthropic) or ChatGPT (OpenAI), the key you paste is stored on your Mac. Spot uses operating-system encryption through Electron safeStorage when available; otherwise it uses base64 encoding, which is not encryption. The key is sent to your chosen provider to validate the connection and run AI features, not to Spot’s proxy.
  • Settings, the sign-in session and the search index. Stored in Spot's own folder under Application Support on your Mac. This includes numeric vectors and cached query vectors when you use the feature described in Article 5. Importer access tokens, such as Are.na or Raindrop tokens, are stored in local settings without safeStorage encryption. Deleting the app’s folder removes these local settings, credentials and indexes; it does not delete your online account.
  • Deleted screenshots. When you delete a screenshot from Spot, it is moved to the macOS Trash, where you can recover it. This screenshot-delete action uses the Trash rather than permanently removing the screenshot.

Article 3 · What is stored online

  • Your account. Signing in with Google gives Spot your Google account's email address, name and profile picture, and creates a login session. Supabase, the authentication service Spot uses, stores this in the United States. It identifies your account and authenticates requests to Spot’s proxy. The current app does not store reference files in Supabase.
  • Usage and eligibility. Spot’s proxy stores account-linked daily embedding request counts, monthly describe counts when that route is used, and Pro eligibility flags. Retention is described in Article 8.
  • Processing content. The proxy application does not persist the reference text, images or vectors it forwards. Requests still pass through hosting and AI providers, whose logging and retention policies apply. This is different from storing a cloud copy of your vault.

Article 4 · Who else receives data

  • Google, to sign you in. Google's privacy policy applies to the sign-in step.
  • Supabase, which stores the account record in Article 3.
  • Anthropic or OpenAI, when you use your own connected key: AI Describe sends the picture and supporting description/tag context; AI widening sends the query and vocabulary. Connecting a key also contacts the provider to validate it. An optional Spot-funded Pro route can forward describe requests through Spot’s proxy to Anthropic, but that route is disabled in the current app configuration. The provider’s policies apply to requests it receives.
  • GitHub, which hosts Spot's downloads and this website. The app checks GitHub for a new version every six hours; that request carries no personal data beyond what any web request carries (your IP address and app version).
  • Cloudflare, which hosts Spot’s proxy for text meaning and the optional Pro describe route.
  • Voyage AI, which receives reference text and queries forwarded by Spot’s text meaning proxy (Article 5).
  • Import sources and linked websites, when you import references or ask Spot to fetch a link or picture. A connected source receives the token needed for that request.

Spot sells no data, shares none for advertising, and contains no analytics, advertising or trackers.

Article 5 · Search by meaning

The text meaning service is deployed, but availability depends on the app version, its proxy configuration and a successfully built local index. It is not enabled in every public release. In a configured copy, Account → Search by meaning → Index now starts indexing manually. The current app does not automatically embed each new save and has no separate meaning or picture consent switches.

Indexing sends text assembled from a reference’s title, full note body, tags grouped by facet, folder names other than excluded source folders, and words read from the picture. Date, image dimensions, orientation and language metadata are kept locally beside the vectors rather than added to this text. The text goes through Spot’s Cloudflare proxy to Voyage AI, which returns numeric embeddings stored on your Mac.

Once a configured copy has a populated index, searches can send query text through the same service. Cached query vectors can avoid a new request. The proxy checks your Supabase sign-in token and counts requests per account; the app and proxy both apply request limits. There is no separate on/off switch in the current implementation.

This embedding path sends text, not pictures. Image embeddings are not implemented. AI Describe is a separate feature that does send a picture to the chosen provider, as described in Article 4. Provider and hosting retention policies still apply; the proxy’s retained usage and eligibility records are described in Article 8.

Article 6 · This website

The website is a set of static pages hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub may log requests to it (IP address, browser, pages fetched) for security and operation under GitHub's privacy statement. When you open the download page, your browser asks GitHub's public API for the newest version number so the page can show it; that request goes to GitHub, not to Spot, and carries nothing from this site. The fonts are served from this site itself. There are no analytics, no advertising, no embedded social widgets and no third-party scripts.

Article 7 · Cookies and similar technologies

This website sets no cookies of its own, uses no local storage in your browser, and uses no tracking pixels or fingerprinting. Because nothing is stored in your browser by this site, there is no cookie banner: there is nothing to consent to.

  • GitHub, as the host, may set cookies of its own when you visit github.com pages such as the downloads page; GitHub's privacy statement governs those.
  • The Spot application stores its settings in a file on your Mac (Article 2), not in cookies.
  • If this site ever starts using cookies or similar technologies, this article will be rewritten first and the page will show a notice before any are set.

Article 8 · Why we may process data, and for how long

  • To run the service you asked for (signing you in, checking for updates, attaching a plan to your account): the account data in Article 3, kept for as long as you have an account.
  • Because you chose it (connecting an AI or starting meaning indexing): the content needed for those requests and, once an index is ready, meaning queries as described in Article 5. You can withdraw consent at any time. You can disconnect your AI key in Account. The current meaning implementation has no separate disable switch; contact the address below to withdraw consent or request help stopping processing and deleting related data.
  • To keep the service safe (rate limits, abuse prevention): daily embedding counts expire 48 hours after their last update; monthly describe counts expire 40 days after their last update. Pro eligibility flags have no automatic expiry in the proxy and remain until removed. Account and related-data deletion requests are covered by Article 9. Hosting logs are kept by GitHub and Cloudflare under their own policies; AI-provider retention is governed by the relevant provider’s policy.

For people in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, those three are the legal bases: performance of a contract, consent, and legitimate interest respectively. Data stored by Supabase and GitHub is held in the United States under their standard contractual clauses.

Article 9 · Your rights and how to delete everything

  • Your references are already yours: plain files in your folder. Nothing to request; take them anywhere.
  • On your Mac: sign out to end the session; delete the app and its folder under Application Support to remove settings, keys and the search index.
  • Your account record: email contentforjason@gmail.com from the address you signed in with and it will be deleted within 30 days, together with any plan and request counts.
  • You can also ask what is held about you, have it corrected, or receive a copy, at the same address. Wherever the law gives you further rights (for example under the GDPR or the California Consumer Privacy Act), you have them, and you may complain to your local data-protection authority.

Article 10 · Security

Keys and the sign-in session use operating-system encryption when safeStorage is available, with the unencrypted base64 fallback described in Article 2. Importer tokens are stored in local settings. The configured authentication and AI services use HTTPS; Google sign-in also returns through an HTTP loopback address on your own computer. Links you ask Spot to fetch may use their own protocols. Spot’s own provider keys belong on its server, not in the downloadable app. Spot is early-access software and no system is perfectly secure; if a breach ever affects your account data, you will be told by email without undue delay.

Article 11 · Children

Spot is not directed at children under 16 and does not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child has created an account, write to the address below and it will be removed.

Article 12 · Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes, the change is listed in the changelog, and the new version is published at this address. Questions, requests and complaints: contentforjason@gmail.com.
